solid → liquid → gas

Overview

Just the essentials — definitions, distinguishing properties, and the laws you need, no lecture filler.

🧊 Solid

  • Particles tightly packed, fixed positions — only vibrate.
  • Fixed shape & volume. Very hard to compress.
  • Lowest rate of diffusion.
  • e.g. ice, sugar, rock, wood.

💧 Liquid

  • Particles less tightly packed than solids; can flow.
  • Fixed volume, no fixed shape — takes container's shape.
  • Hard to compress; diffusion faster than solids.
  • e.g. water, milk, blood, coffee.

💨 Gas

  • Particles far apart, negligible attraction, move freely.
  • No fixed shape or volume. Highest compressibility.
  • Highest diffusion rate & kinetic energy.
  • e.g. air, He, N₂, O₂, CO₂.

🔗 Binding Forces

  • Intramolecular = bonds holding atoms together in a molecule (covalent).
  • Intermolecular = attraction/repulsion between molecules (needed for gas/liquid/solid aggregates).
  • Van der Waals = weak, distance-dependent, nonionic but involve charge fluctuations.
  • Hydrogen bond = H attracted to strongly electronegative F, O, N — explains water's high dielectric constant, low vapor pressure, high boiling point.

📐 Gas Laws

Boyle's: PV = k (const T)

Charles': V = kT (const P)

Combined: P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂

Ideal gas: PV = nRT

M = gRT / PV
  • R = 0.08205 L·atm/mol·K (or 8.314 J/mol·K)
  • 0 °C = 273.15 K · 1 atm = 760 mmHg

🌫️ Transitions

  • Sublimation: solid → gas directly (iodine, camphor).
  • Deposition: gas → solid directly (reverse of sublimation).
  • Heating a solid → atoms gain energy → disrupt lattice → liquid → eventually gas.
  • Real gas ≈ ideal gas at high T, low P (particles have volume & interact in a real gas; ideal gas particles have no volume & no interactions).
